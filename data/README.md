# Sample production data

`sample_wells.csv` is generated by `decline_curve_lab.synthetic` from a fixed seed and
committed to the repository so the dashboard has something to show on a fresh clone.

## Seed

    DEFAULT_SEED = 20260902

Regenerate the file with:

    PYTHONPATH=src /tmp/opencode/dcl-venv/bin/python -m decline_curve_lab.synthetic

(once the package is installed, `python -m decline_curve_lab.synthetic` on its own is
enough). Pass a different seed with `--seed`.

The generator is fully deterministic: the same seed always produces byte-identical
output, so a regeneration that differs byte-for-byte is a change to the generator, not
noise. `tests/test_synthetic.py` asserts exactly this against the committed file, and
fails with "regenerate it" if the two drift apart. Pass a different seed with
`--seed`.

Each well's measurement noise comes from its own stream, derived from the seed *and*
the well id, so changing one well's parameters leaves every other well's numbers
untouched.

## Units — rates are daily averages

Every rate in this file is a **daily average over the production period, never the
volume produced during it** (ADR-0002, `docs/adr/0002-rates-are-daily-averages.md`).

| column | unit |
|---|---|
| `date` | production period start date, ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` |
| `well_id` | well identifier |
| `qo` | **bbl/d, daily average** oil rate |
| `qw` | **bbl/d, daily average** water rate |
| `qg` | **scf/d, daily average** gas rate |
| `wellhead_pressure` | psi |
| `choke` | inches |

One row is one **production period**: one month, for one well. Elapsed time `t` counts
months from each well's *own* first production period, starting at `t = 0` — the wells
start in different calendar months, so a shared `t` would be meaningless.

A rate of `100` in `qo` means the well made 100 barrels of oil **on an average day**,
not 100 barrels in the month. `decline_curve_lab.io.load_production` documents and
enforces this: it reads rates exactly as written and never rescales them.

`Di` is the nominal decline per **year** as a fraction (ADR-0001), the generator's
ground truth; the fitter recovers it as a fitted `qi`/`Di`. `b` is the Arps decline
curvature. The terminal-switch column is where the decline reaches the EIA terminal
decline of 10 %/yr nominal.

## Cases the wells were built to provide

Later tickets depend on these being unambiguous, so they were designed deliberately:

- **Low rate and high water cut together — `DCL-04` only.** It is the only well with
  any production period where `qo < 100 bbl/d` *and* water cut `> 0.7`; it holds that
  state for 31 consecutive production periods, from 2024-03 to 2026-09.
- **Sustained wellhead-pressure decline — `DCL-03` only.** Its wellhead pressure falls
  in **every one** of its 36 production periods (−3.3 %/period, 245 → 72 psi), while the
  well stays above 100 bbl/d with water cut below 0.7. The pressure signal is therefore
  isolated from the other two signals. No other well declines in wellhead pressure at
  all: the rest hold roughly steady and build slightly, so a screening rule separates
  `DCL-03` on monotonicity, on a magnitude threshold, or on both.
- **Exponential — `DCL-02`.** Decline curvature `b = 0`, shallow (`Di = 0.18/yr`), the
  longest history at 72 production periods. Only well to dip below 100 bbl/d late, and
  it does so with water cut still at 0.23.
- **Steep hyperbolic — `DCL-01`.** Highest rate and steepest decline (`Di = 0.62/yr`,
  `b = 0.85`), never reaching either screening threshold.
- **Harmonic — `DCL-06`.** `b = 1`, kept because the harmonic curve is a distinct
  member of the Arps family, **not** a limit of the exponential.
- **Liquid-loading control — `DCL-05`.** Wellhead pressure builds, gas-oil ratio *falls*
  (893 → 514 scf/bbl), and it stays above 100 bbl/d with water cut below 0.25
  throughout. The well no screening rule should flag.

## Two things worth knowing before fitting

1. **The terminal switch falls outside every history here.** With `b` in [0, 1], the
   hyperbolic reaches the 10 %/yr terminal decline 8 to 17 years out, well past these
   3-to-6-year histories. A forecast over these wells will normally be bounded by the
   economic limit rate (ADR-0003) rather than by the terminal switch. This is the
   expected behaviour for well-behaved `b < 1` wells, not a gap in the sample.
2. **Choke is a derived proxy, not an operational log.** It is quantised to 1/64 in and
   computed from each well's noise-free oil rate as a stand-in for deliverability, so it
   steps down in fixed increments and does not flap under measurement noise.
3. **Measured water cut only approximates each well's specified water cut.** `qo` and
   `qw` each carry independent measurement noise (±1.5 %, or ±1 % for `DCL-03`), so
   water cut recovered from the data lands within ±0.01 of the table's figure rather
   than matching it exactly. The `fw` column above is the noise-free value the
   generator aimed for.
